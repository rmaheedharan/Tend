# Why nothing in Tend ever counts against you

*Explanation · Requirements: FR-B2, UX-3, FR-B3 · Built in M1 (engine only; the screens arrive in M3)*

Weeks go sideways. When a task's time passes before you get to it, Tend does not keep score. It quietly finds the
next good time and moves on. We call this compassionate rescheduling.

## What happens when a task's time passes

Tend looks at what kind of task it is:

- **A deep clean** (like cleaning the oven) becomes a lighter version. It counts as a light task, so it can fit even
  a low-energy day. A quick tidy counts.
- **Something important** (like paying a bill, or food that will not keep) comes back gently. It goes to the top of
  your three at its next good time. It never gets louder, however many times it comes back.
- **Anything else** simply moves to its next good time: the next part of the day and day of the week that suit it.

A task keeps the same amount of time it had before. If you gave a task a week, it gets another week.

If you asked Tend to rest a task until a later date, Tend leaves it alone until then.

## What Tend never does

- It never keeps a count of how many times a task moved.
- It never remembers the original date so it could work out how long ago it was.
- It never adds a score that grows the longer something waits.
- It never changes a task's tone. Any reminder about it stays gentle.

Tend also cannot do these things by accident. The data it stores has no place for them, and automatic checks run on
every change to make sure no one adds one.

## When you finish a task

- A one-time task or a restock is marked done.
- A repeating task starts again at its next good time after its interval, at the same time of day.
- The room it belongs to is marked as tended now. Later, Room Pulse will use this to show which rooms feel cared for.
